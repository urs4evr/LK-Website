Relationship tracker: free love trackers and what to avoid

A relationship tracker, often called a love tracker, is an app or website that keeps your relationship dates in one place. Most count how long you have been together, show your next anniversary and remind you before important days. Some also log memories, moods or check-ins. Tracking your partner's location is a different thing, and it needs their consent.

What does a love tracker actually track?

A love tracker tracks your shared dates and the time between them. The word covers several kinds of tools, though, and most confusion comes from mixing them up. This table shows what each kind does and when it helps.

The first three are what most people mean by "relationship tracker online". Mood trackers and shared couple apps work best when both partners want them. Location sharing is not about your relationship's story at all, so it gets its own section below.

Yes. Free web tools can count your time together and list your coming milestones, with nothing to install. They are the fastest way to answer "how long have we been together?" or "when is our 1,000th day?".

  • Count your time together: the relationship counter shows days, months and years, your next anniversary and your next round number. You can share a link so your partner sees the same count.
  • See every coming milestone: the anniversary calculator lists the exact dates of your 100 days, 6 months, 1,000 days, 5 years and more.

A website has one limit: it cannot remind you. It only works when you open it. If you want a nudge a week before your anniversary, you need an app or a calendar entry.

What should a good relationship tracker do?

A good relationship tracker gets your dates right, reminds you early enough to plan, and is clear about price and data. Use these five checks before you download anything.

  1. Correct date rules. Months should fall on the same day number as your start date, and a start on the 31st needs a rule for shorter months. Some apps count your first day as day 1, others as day 0, so two apps can show different totals.
  2. Reminders before the day. A reminder on the day itself is often too late for a booking or a gift. Look for alerts several days or weeks ahead.
  3. One person can use it. Some couple apps only work after both partners create accounts and pair them. If your partner is not into apps, pick one that works for one person.
  4. An honest price. Many apps called "love tracker" in the App Store are free to download but list in-app purchases or subscriptions. Check the "In-App Purchases" line in the listing before you add your dates.
  5. Clear data handling. Know whether your dates and photos stay on your phone or go to a server, and whether you can export a backup.

A home screen widget is a plus if you like to see your count every time you unlock your phone. Not every tracker has one, and the comparison of the best anniversary apps shows which do.

Should you track your partner's location?

Only if you both agree and either of you can stop at any time. Sharing locations openly, for example through Find My on iPhone or location sharing in Google Maps, can help on a long drive or a late trip home. Secretly tracking a partner is something else: it is surveillance, not a relationship tracker.

Some sites and apps use the word "tracker" for exactly that. Be careful when a tool:

  • asks for your partner's phone number to "scan hidden databases" or find dating profiles,
  • offers a stealth or hidden mode that runs without your partner knowing,
  • promises to show another person's messages, calls or social media activity.

In the US, secretly installing monitoring software on another adult's phone can break federal and state laws on wiretapping and stalking. The Federal Trade Commission banned the maker of the SpyFone app from the surveillance business in 2021. If the urge to check comes from distrust, a direct conversation, or a couples counselor, helps more than any app.

If you think someone is tracking you without consent, the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) can help you plan safely, including what to check on your phone.

How do you start tracking your relationship?

You can set up a relationship tracker in about ten minutes. Start with the dates, then decide how you want to be reminded.

  1. Agree on your start date. Most couples use their first date or the day they became official. If you are not sure, old messages and photo dates usually show it.
  2. Add the dates that matter to you both. First kiss, moving in, your wedding, each other's birthdays. Skip the ones neither of you cares about.
  3. Check your next milestones. Enter your start date in the anniversary calculator to see when your next round number falls.
  4. Set reminders ahead of time. One week before for a dinner booking, two to four weeks before if you plan a trip or an engraved gift.
  5. Attach a memory. One photo or one sentence per date is enough, and it is what you will enjoy reading later.

For long-distance couples, counting down to the next visit can matter more than counting up. The long-distance relationship apps guide covers apps for calls, shared games and visit countdowns.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free love tracker app?

Yes. Many love tracker apps are free to download, but a lot of them list in-app purchases, subscriptions or ads in their store listing. LoveKeeper is free with no subscription, no in-app purchases and no ads, and only one partner needs it.

Can I see how many days we have been together online?

Yes. A free web counter such as the LoveKeeper relationship counter shows your time together in days, months and years as soon as you enter your start date. It needs no account, and the date stays in your browser.

Is it okay to track your partner's phone?

Only if your partner knows and agrees, and can turn it off at any time. Secretly installing monitoring software on another adult's phone can break wiretapping and stalking laws in the US, and it damages trust more than it builds it.

What is the best relationship tracker app?

It depends on what you want to track. For dates and reminders, pick an app that alerts you days ahead and does not require both partners to sign up. For a home screen counter, pick one with a widget. The comparison of the best anniversary apps lists price, widgets and partner requirements for ten apps.

Does my partner need the app too?

Not with every app. Some couple apps only work once both partners join and pair their accounts. Others, such as LoveKeeper, work fully for one person, so you can track your dates without asking your partner to install anything.
