Sex Bucket List: 40 Best Ideas and How to Make One

A sex bucket list is a short, shared list of intimate things a couple wants to try, repeat or plan together. Each partner suggests ideas privately, you keep only the ones you both say yes to, and you tick them off over months, not in one weekend. The point is curiosity and connection, not a performance checklist.

How do you make a sex bucket list together?

You make a sex bucket list in three steps: write ideas alone, sort them together, and keep only the mutual yeses. Writing alone first matters most. It lets each of you name a wish without watching the other person's face.

  1. Write privately for ten minutes. Each of you notes anything you are curious about, from small ("a slow massage with the phone in another room") to bigger ("a weekend away just for us"). Use the ideas further down if you get stuck.
  2. Sort every idea into yes, maybe or no. Swap lists and mark each of your partner's ideas. Do it on a relaxed evening, not in bed and not right after an argument.
  3. Keep the overlap. Ideas you both mark yes go on the shared list. Maybes stay on a separate "talk about it" list. Nobody explains or defends a no.

Then pick one item for the next two to four weeks and plan it like a date: when, where, what you need. A list without a next step stays a list.

What should you put on a sex bucket list?

Put things on the list you can actually plan, in a mix of easy and adventurous. Specific entries work better than vague ones: "breakfast in bed, then nowhere to be until noon" is something you can schedule, "be more spontaneous" is not.

A good list usually has three kinds of entries:

  • Easy wins you could do this week, to build momentum.
  • Bigger plans that need booking or saving, like a trip or a hotel night.
  • Conversations, such as a fantasy you want to talk about before you decide anything.

Keep adding ideas when they come up. A sex bucket list for couples changes as you do, and that is normal.

40 sex bucket list ideas for couples

These ideas lean romantic and playful, with a few that need more trust. Read them together and mark each one yes, maybe or no. Skip anything that does not fit you, and change details until an idea feels like yours.

Slow and sensual

  1. A full-body massage with warm oil, no time limit.
  2. An evening where touch is the only plan and nobody rushes.
  3. A blindfold for one of you, for touch and taste only.
  4. A bath or shower together by candlelight.
  5. A morning in bed with breakfast, phones off until noon.
  6. A night of only kissing, as if it were your first months.
  7. A playlist you both build for one evening.
  8. Getting dressed up for each other at home.

Playful

  1. A dice or card game that decides what happens next. The free sex dice game on BondAmour works on any phone.
  2. Strip version of a game you already like.
  3. A dare jar you both fill and draw from once a week.
  4. Role play as strangers who meet at a bar. The sexual roleplay game gives you scenarios if you need a start.
  5. One evening where one partner plans everything and the other only says yes or no.
  6. Trading roles: whoever usually initiates waits this time.
  7. A "yes, maybe, no" night with a printed list and a drink (or tea).
  8. Trying one new position you both choose beforehand.

New places

  1. A hotel night in your own city.
  2. A cabin or rental with a fireplace.
  3. A different room at home, or the same room rearranged.
  4. A tent, sleeping bag and a clear night.
  5. A road trip with one overnight stop you pick at random.
  6. A spa day that ends in your room.
  7. A beach or lake trip with a private place to stay.
  8. A return to the place you first stayed together.

Talk and tease

  1. Telling each other one fantasy, with no promise to act on it.
  2. A flirty text thread during a normal workday.
  3. A handwritten note hidden where your partner will find it.
  4. Describing your best memory together in detail.
  5. Trying dirty talk with one sentence each, then laughing about it.
  6. Asking "what did you like most?" after an evening together.
  7. Reading erotic fiction aloud, one page each.
  8. Planning a surprise for each other with one hint per day.

Bigger adventures

  1. A lingerie or underwear shopping trip together.
  2. A boudoir or couple photo shoot with a photographer you trust, or photos only for yourselves.
  3. A dance class with close contact, such as salsa or tango.
  4. A weekend without any plan except each other.
  5. A couples massage class.
  6. Choosing a toy together.
  7. A trip abroad with one evening kept free from sightseeing.
  8. Repeating the best night from this list on your anniversary.

Ideas that involve other people, public places or new health risks need a longer conversation than a checkbox. If anything on your list touches sexual health, such as new partners or contraception, talk to a doctor or a clinic before you plan it.

How do you talk about a sex bucket list without pressure?

You talk about it without pressure by agreeing on the rules before you share any ideas. Couples who skip this step often turn a fun list into a negotiation.

These four rules keep it light:

  • Every no is final for now. No "why not?" and no asking again the same week. The person who said no can bring it back later.
  • A maybe needs a condition. "Maybe, if we go slow" or "maybe, after we talk about it more" tells your partner what would help.
  • Nobody owes an item. A yes on the list means "I'd like to try this", not "I agreed, so I have to".
  • Check in during and after. A short "still good?" and a "what did you like?" afterwards make the next item easier.

Consent can change at any moment, even halfway through something you planned for weeks. Stopping is always fine, and it does not need a reason.

If one of you wants a lot more than the other, the list is not the place to settle it. Start with the overlap and keep the rest for a calm conversation. Different wishes are not a verdict on your relationship.

How often should you revisit your sex bucket list?

Revisit your list about once a month, and right after you tick something off. A short look keeps it alive without making it a chore.

A simple rhythm:

  • After each item: two minutes on what you liked and whether you want it again.
  • Once a month: pick the next item, move maybes up or down, delete what no longer appeals.
  • On anniversaries: look at the completed items together. Many couples enjoy this more than the list itself. The guide to relationship milestones has more dates worth marking.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a sex bucket list?

Anything you both want to try or repeat: a new place, a slower kind of evening, a game, a fantasy you want to talk about, a trip. Good entries are specific enough to plan, like "a weekend in a hotel with no phones" instead of "more romance".

How do you start a sex bucket list with your partner?

Each of you writes ideas privately first, then you sort them into yes, maybe and no, and only the ideas you both mark yes go on the shared list. Start with low-stakes items so the first conversation feels easy.

What if my partner says no to something on my list?

Treat a no as information, not rejection. Thank them, drop the item without negotiating, and ask what would feel good instead. A no today can become a maybe later, but only if they bring it up.

How many items should a sex bucket list have?

Five to fifteen shared items is enough to start. A short list gets used, and you can add new ideas every few weeks. Long lists tend to feel like homework.

Where can I keep a sex bucket list privately?

Somewhere only you can open, such as an app on your locked phone. In LoveKeeper, bucket list items are stored on your device, you can use neutral titles and emojis, and you need no account.
