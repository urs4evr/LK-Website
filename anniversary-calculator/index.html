    How does the anniversary calculator work?

    The calculator adds whole years, whole months or a set number of days to your date and shows the results that are still in the future. Yearly anniversaries and monthly milestones keep your day number. Day counts such as 100 or 1,000 days are counted day by day, so they land on a different date for every couple.

    It lists these milestones, sorted by date:

    • Your next anniversary: always shown, whether it is your 1st, your 12th or your 37th.
    • Round years: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 50 years.
    • Months: 1, 3 and 6 months, for new couples.
    • Day counts: 100, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000 and 10,000 days.

    The calculator works for any date: a wedding, the day you became a couple, a first date, a work anniversary or the day a friendship began. Choose “Our marriage” to add your wedding date and, optionally, your dating date. The calculator shows both journeys together. Future dates work too, for a wedding you are planning.

    How do you calculate how many years you have been married?

    Subtract your wedding year from the current year. If this year's anniversary has not happened yet, subtract one more. Someone married on June 21, 2014 has been married 12 years on November 2, 2026, and the 13th anniversary follows on June 21, 2027.

    The same rule tells you which anniversary is coming next: it is the number of full years plus one. On your anniversary itself, the calculator celebrates it as “Today”. The following day, it starts counting down to the next year.

    When does an anniversary fall in a shorter month?

    If your date is the 29th, 30th or 31st, a month without that day uses its last day instead. A couple who got together on January 31 reaches one month on February 28, or February 29 in a leap year, and three months on April 30. In months that have the 31st, the date returns to the 31st.

    This matters mostly for monthly milestones, because yearly anniversaries keep the same month. The calculator applies the rule for you. What each monthly milestone means and how couples mark it is covered in relationship milestones and monthsaries.

    What if your anniversary is on February 29?

    A February 29 date has a real anniversary only in leap years: 2028, 2032 and 2036 are the next ones. In other years, this calculator uses February 28, so the date stays in February. There is no official rule, and many couples pick March 1 instead. Choose one together and keep it.

    Day counts are not affected. Your 1,000th day is 1,000 days after February 29, like for anyone else.

    What day of the week will your anniversary fall on?

    Your anniversary moves one weekday later each year, and two weekdays when a February 29 falls in between. A June 20 anniversary was a Thursday in 2019, a Saturday in 2020 because of the leap day, and a Sunday in 2021. The calculator shows the weekday next to every date, so you can see early which year lands on a weekend.

    That helps with planning. A round anniversary on a Saturday is easy to celebrate on the day. One on a Tuesday is often better moved to the weekend before or after, with something small on the day itself.

    When is your 100-day and 1,000-day anniversary?

    Your 100-day anniversary falls 3 months and 8 to 11 days after your start date. Your 1,000-day anniversary falls 2 years, 8 months and 24 to 28 days after it. The exact gap depends on which months and leap days lie in between, which is why the calculator counts them day by day.

    Whether a one-month anniversary is worth marking is up to the two of you; some couples love it, others count only years. If you are unsure, mention the date a few days before.

    How do you calculate an anniversary in Excel or Google Sheets?

    Put your date in cell A2. Both Excel and Google Sheets understand these formulas:

    You wantFormula
    Full years since the date=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")
    Full months since the date=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "M")
    Days since the date=TODAY()-A2
    Date of the next anniversary=EDATE(A2, 12*(DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")+1))
    Days until the next anniversary=EDATE(A2, 12*(DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")+1))-TODAY()
    Date of day 1,000=A2+1000

    Format the date results as dates, not numbers. EDATE keeps your day number and uses the last day of a shorter month, the same rule as the calculator above. Results for a February 29 date can differ by a day between programs, so check those against the calculator.

    How do you make sure you never forget your anniversary?

    Save the date once with a reminder well before the day, not only on it. A reminder on the day leaves no time to plan a dinner or order something. Two weeks before gives you room to prepare, and one more on the day makes sure you say it.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I work out which anniversary it is?

    Subtract the year you got together or married from the current year. If that date has already passed this year, that number is the anniversary you last celebrated. If it is still to come, it is the one coming up.

    Is a one-month anniversary the same as 30 days?

    No. A one-month anniversary falls on the same day number in the next month, which is 28 to 31 days later. If your date is the 31st, it falls on the last day of a shorter month.

    What if our date is February 29?

    In years without a February 29, this calculator uses February 28. Many couples celebrate on February 28 or March 1, so choose whichever feels right.

    How many years is 1,000 days?

    1,000 days is 2 years, 8 months and 24 to 28 days, depending on your start date. It is a little less than 2 years and 9 months.

    Can I use the calculator for a work anniversary?

    Yes. Enter your start date at work and the calculator shows your next work anniversary and round dates such as 5 or 10 years and 1,000 days.

    How do I find out how long we have been together?

    Use the relationship counter. It shows your time together in days, weeks, months and years.
