Relationship milestones: the dates and firsts worth celebrating

Relationship milestones are the dates and firsts a couple chooses to mark. Some you can count, such as 1 month, 100 days, 6 months and your first anniversary. Others are moments, such as the first "I love you", meeting the family or moving in. There is no required order. Mark the ones that matter to you both.

Which relationship milestones can you count by date?

The milestones you can count are monthly ones (1, 3 and 6 months), yearly anniversaries and round day counts such as 100, 500 and 1,000 days. Months and years fall on the same day number as your start date. Day counts land on a different date for every couple, so they are easy to miss without working them out.

Two rules decide the exact date. If you got together on the 29th, 30th or 31st, a shorter month uses its last day, so January 31 becomes February 28 or 29. If you got together on February 29, most years use February 28. The anniversary calculator applies both rules and also lists 2,000, 5,000 and 10,000 days.

Which date counts as your anniversary?

Your dating anniversary is whichever date the two of you agree on. Most couples use either their first date or the day they became official. Both are fine. What matters is that you pick the same one.

  • First date: clear and easy to remember, even if you were not a couple yet.
  • The day you became official: the talk where you agreed to be exclusive. It often feels more meaningful but is harder to pin down.
  • No clear date: choose one together, such as your first kiss or the first trip, and keep it from then on.

Some couples mark both dates, and some keep their dating anniversary after they marry. Old messages and photos usually show the exact day if neither of you remembers it.

What is a monthsary?

A monthsary is the monthly anniversary of a relationship. The word blends "month" and "anniversary" and comes from Philippine English, where couples often mark every month together. It is not a fixed number of days: if you got together on March 12, every 12th is your monthsary, whether the month has 28 or 31 days.

Other words for it are "monthiversary" or simply "happy 3 months". Major print dictionaries such as Merriam-Webster do not list "monthsary", but it is widely understood. Your first monthsary is the day one month after you got together.

A monthsary works best when it stays small. A few lines you can send or say:

  • "Happy monthsary. Same day, new month, still you."
  • "One month down, and I would pick you again."
  • "Three months of us. Thank you for every one of those days."
  • "Half a year already. Let's do something on Saturday, just the two of us."

Do people celebrate 1, 3 and 6 months?

Some couples do, and opinions are split. Some people find a one-month anniversary sweet, while others feel anniversaries only count in years. Instead of guessing, mention it a few days before, so neither of you is caught off guard with a gift.

Keep the first months light: a message, a favorite meal or a short walk back to where you met. Why do couples celebrate 100 and 1,000 days?

Couples celebrate 100 and 1,000 days because round numbers feel special and land on dates you would not otherwise notice. In South Korea, the 100-day anniversary (baek-il) is a well-known custom. It borrows from the traditional celebration of a baby's 100th day. Many Korean couples keep counting in hundreds, to 200, 300 and 1,000 days, and some buy matching couple rings around day 100.

What are the big relationship milestones besides dates?

The big relationship milestones besides dates are firsts and decisions: becoming official, saying "I love you", meeting each other's people, getting through a first fight, traveling together and building a shared life. They roughly fall into three stages, though few couples hit them in the same order.

Early on

  • First date and first kiss
  • The "what are we?" talk, when you become exclusive or official
  • First sleepover and first nicknames for each other
  • First "I love you"

Getting closer

  • Meeting each other's friends, then family
  • Your first real argument and how you make up. The repair says more about you as a couple than the fight does.
  • Letting the other person see you at a low point, or crying in front of each other
  • First trip and first major holiday together
  • Keys to each other's place

Building a life together

  • Moving in together
  • Getting a pet
  • Talking openly about money and long-term plans
  • Engagement, wedding or another kind of commitment
  • Buying a home

Not every couple wants all of these. A relationship without a wedding, a shared home or a pet can be just as committed.

When do couples usually reach these milestones?

Most couples say "I love you" and meet the family within the first six months, and most who move in or get engaged do it after the first year. That is what a YouGov poll of more than 1,300 US adults found in August 2021. It asked couples how long they had been dating when they reached each milestone.

In the same poll, 17% of men but only 9% of women said "I love you" is fine within the first month. So there is no schedule to keep up with. What matters is that you both feel ready.

How do you make a relationship timeline?

A relationship timeline is a list of your milestones with dates, so you can look back and see the next one coming. You can build one in an evening:

  1. Write down the firsts you remember, and ask your partner to add theirs. You will remember different things.
  2. Find exact dates in old messages, photo metadata and calendar entries.
  3. Add one photo or one sentence to each date. Short is fine.
  4. Keep it somewhere you will open again, such as a shared album, a printed photo book or an app.
  5. Add the next milestones from the calculator above, so you see them coming.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 3-6-9 rule in relationships?

It is a dating-advice rule of thumb that splits the first nine months into three stages: about three months of honeymoon, three months of conflict as differences show, and three months of deciding whether to commit. It is not a research finding, and many couples move at a different pace.

What is the 7-7-7 rule in relationships?

It is a routine some couples use to protect time together: a date every 7 days, a night or weekend away every 7 weeks, and a longer trip every 7 months. Treat the numbers as a prompt and adjust them to your schedule and budget.

What are the 5 stages of a relationship?

A common model comes from Susan Campbell's book The Couple's Journey: romance, power struggle, stability, commitment and co-creation. Other writers use different names and numbers of stages, and couples can move back and forth between them.

What is a 2-year anniversary called?

It has no special name. Most people say 2-year anniversary or second anniversary. For wedding anniversaries, the traditional theme for year two is cotton.
